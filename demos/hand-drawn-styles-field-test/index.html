What the Skill actually changed

Not just “make it hand drawn.” The workflow turns style selection into a small, inspectable production system.

  1. 01Select

    Choose one named recipe from the gallery instead of mixing visual adjectives.

  2. 02Render exact

    Extract the verified block with the supplied renderer, preserving every constraint.

  3. 03Fill

    Replace only the recipe placeholders: five panels, five actions, one visual story.

  4. 04Generate + inspect

    Hand the complete prompt to an image model, then check character, hierarchy, color, and text.

A hand-drawn black marker doodle explainer illustration on off-white paper,
vertical infographic with 5 stacked panels separated by thin hand-drawn lines.

Character: a simple solid-black round blob person with two white dot eyes,
a small curved smile, and thin black stick arms and legs. The body is solid
flat black with smooth edges (no sketchy texture). The SAME character appears
in every panel doing a different action.

Style: clean black marker/pen outlines, slightly wobbly but fully closed
hand-drawn lines. All objects are OUTLINE-ONLY black line-art with white
interiors, no shading, no fill. Everything is monochrome black & white EXCEPT
one orange accent color used ONLY for the single key item in each panel.
Hand-drawn curved arrows in red or blue point at key spots, each with a short
handwritten Chinese label. Each panel has a bold hand-written marker-style
Chinese title at the top. Minimalist composition, lots of white space.

Inspect the source ↗

Three things it does well

  1. The 19 recipes are concrete and gallery-backed, so their differences are easy to choose.
  2. The renderer preserves the verified recipe and validates placeholders, reducing prompt drift.
  3. Style 4 produced a coherent five-panel story with a stable character and strong hierarchy.

Three things to improve

  1. The large menu needs a faster task-to-style recommendation path.
  2. Image-model text can drift from requested English content to recipe-mandated Chinese labels.
  3. Style 4 says orange-only, then permits red or blue arrows, creating a palette contradiction.
